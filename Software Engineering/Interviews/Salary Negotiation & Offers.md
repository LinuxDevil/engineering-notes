# Salary Negotiation & Offers

<Note>
**TL;DR**

An offer is a package, not a salary: base, bonus, equity, sign-on, level, and benefits. Your leverage comes from **alternatives** (competing offers, a good current job) and **information** (market data for your level and location). Don't name a number first if you can avoid it, always negotiate politely once the offer is written, negotiate the **level** as well as the money, and get everything in writing. This note is general career guidance, not financial or tax advice.
</Note>

## Anatomy of an offer

| Component | What it is | Questions to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Base salary** | Fixed yearly pay | Band for this level? When are reviews? |
| **Bonus** | Annual target % of base, depends on company/individual performance | Target %, historical payout rate |
| **Equity** | RSUs (public companies) or stock options (startups) | Grant value, **vesting schedule** (e.g. 4 years, 1-year cliff, or front-loaded), refreshers |
| **Sign-on bonus** | One-off cash, often with a clawback if you leave within 12 months | Clawback terms; can it cover unvested equity you lose? |
| **Level / title** | Determines band, scope and future raises | How was the level decided? What would the next level need? |
| **Benefits** | Pension/401k match, health, leave, learning budget, remote policy | Remote/hybrid rules in writing |
| **Start date & relocation** | Flexibility and support | Relocation package, visa support |

**Total compensation (TC)** per year ≈ base + expected bonus + (equity grant ÷ vesting years) + (sign-on amortised). Compare offers on the same basis.

<Info>
**Comparing two offers (illustrative numbers)**

| | Company A (public) | Company B (startup) |
|---|---|---|
| Base | 150k | 165k |
| Bonus | 15% target → 22.5k | none |
| Equity | 200k RSUs / 4 yrs → 50k/yr | 0.1% options, strike 2/share |
| Sign-on | 20k (year 1) | 0 |
| Year-1 TC | ≈ 242k | 165k cash + options of uncertain value |

Startup options are worth something only if there is a liquidity event above the strike price; value them conservatively (many treat them as a lottery ticket) and ask about the latest preferred price, number of shares outstanding, exercise window and dilution.
</Info>

## The process

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  A[Recruiter asks expectations] --> B[Deflect politely / give researched range late]
  B --> C[Verbal offer]
  C --> D[Ask for written details<br/>thank, don't accept yet]
  D --> E[Counter with data & alternatives]
  E --> F[Revised offer]
  F --> G[Decide · get final in writing]
```

### 1. Before the offer: research
- Market data by company, level and location: Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Blind, national salary surveys, the Stack Overflow Developer Survey salary section, and pay-transparency postings (many US states and the EU Pay Transparency Directive require ranges; EU member states must transpose it by June 2026).
- Know your **walk-away number** and your **target**.
- Line up interviews in parallel so offers arrive together. Competing offers are the strongest leverage.

### 2. When asked "What are your salary expectations?"
- Early in the process: *"I'd like to learn more about the role and level first. I'm sure we can find a number that works if it's a fit. What's the band for this role?"*
- If you must give a number: give a **researched range** whose bottom is a number you'd be happy with, and say it's for total compensation.
- Current salary: in many places employers may not ask (salary-history bans in several US states). You can decline: *"I'd prefer to focus on the value of this role."*

### 3. When the offer arrives
- Thank them, show enthusiasm, ask for the full details in writing, and ask for time (a few days is normal).
- Never accept on the call.

### 4. The counter
Script:
> *"Thanks again, I'm excited about the team. I have another offer at around X total compensation, and based on the scope we discussed and market data for this level, I was hoping for something closer to Y. If you can get to Y, I'm ready to sign."*

- Anchor on **total compensation**, then let them choose the lever (base, equity, sign-on).
- Be specific and honest. Never invent competing offers.
- Negotiate the **level** if the interviews went well: a level difference often matters more than 10% of base.
- One or two rounds is normal. Stay friendly; the recruiter is usually your advocate with the compensation team.

### 5. Deciding
Beyond money: the manager, the team's scope, growth path, on-call load, remote policy, company stability. A slightly lower offer with a much better manager can be the better career move.

## Pitfalls
<Warning>
**Common mistakes**

- Naming a low number first, which anchors everything after it.
- Negotiating only base and ignoring equity, level and sign-on.
- Accepting on the spot, then trying to negotiate.
- Bluffing about offers or deadlines. Recruiters talk, and it can cost the offer.
- Treating startup options like cash.
- Being aggressive or ultimatum-driven. Firm and friendly works better.
- Forgetting the clawback terms on a sign-on bonus or relocation.
</Warning>

## Senior perspective
- At senior and staff level, **level and scope** drive long-term earnings. Ask what separates the offered level from the next one and ask for the higher level if your loop supported it.
- Ask about **refreshers** and promotion history; a big initial grant with no refreshers creates a pay cliff in year 5.
- If you are losing unvested equity or a bonus by leaving, ask for a sign-on bonus to "make you whole".
- For internal raises and promotions, the same rules apply: data, documented impact (a brag document), and timing with review cycles.

## Interview questions
<Accordion title="What are your salary expectations?">
Deflect politely early and ask for the band. If pressed, give a researched total-compensation range for the level and location, with the bottom at a number you'd accept happily.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why are you leaving your current job?">
Positive and forward-looking: the scope, technology or growth this role offers. Never criticise your current employer.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What matters most to you in an offer?">
Be honest about priorities (scope, team, growth, compensation, flexibility). It helps the recruiter shape the offer; it's not a trap.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do you value equity from a startup vs a public company?">
Public RSUs: value at current share price minus some volatility discount. Startup options: value depends on a future exit, strike price, preferences and dilution; assume a large chance of zero and ask for the numbers needed to estimate it.
</Accordion>
